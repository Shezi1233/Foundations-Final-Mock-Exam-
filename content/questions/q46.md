A water-taxi operator’s associate has Claude draft a high-stakes safety memo, then asks a second Claude model to review it and calls this cross-model checking. She plans to treat the review’s score as proof that the memo is right.

**What is the problem with this check?**

A. Two models are too few for a safety memo; at least four are needed.
B. Cross-model checking is pointless because every model is trained on the same data.
C. Models of one family share blind spots, so use another family.
D. Nothing is wrong, since a second model reviewing the first is enough here.

## Explanation

Cross-model checking works across different families, because models of one family share habits and blind spots. The score is a progress signal, not a truth signal.
